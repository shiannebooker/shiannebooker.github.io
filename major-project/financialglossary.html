    • Financial Glossary

Okay future saver, here are some propably very confusing terms. You might not know what they mean. Here is your Financial Glossary.

  • Budget: A plan for how you will spend your income. Can be weekly, daily, monthly, etc.
  • Income: Any money that you earn. Either from a job, side gig, etc.
  • Expenses: Money that you spend on a regular basis. This is catagorized with needs and wants.
  • Savings: Money that you choose to set aside instead of spending. This later can be used for emergency expenses.
  • Debt: Money that you owe to someone or a company. i.e. Loans, Credit cards. Debt more often than not grows, with interest if not paid back in a timely manner.
  • Interest: The money that is added to your debt monthly/yearly, by a percentage.
  • Credit score: A 3 digit number that tells companys how reliable you are at paying back debt. The higher the number, the more opportunities might become availible to you. (Renting an apartment, Buying a car, Getting a loan)
  • Credit Card: A Company gives you a card with a limit on it. You can spend that money, But you are expected to pay it back.
  • Credit Limit: The limit you are allowed to spend on a credit card.
  • Minimum Payment: The smallest amount that you can pay back monthly on your debt.
  • Fixed Expense: An expense price that does not change.
  • Variable Expense: An expense price that changes based on usage.
  • Net income: The amount of money you take home after taxes & deductions on your paycheck.
  • Gross Income: The amount of money fully earned BEFORE taxes & deductions. (Hence the name gross, because it's gross seeing the amount of money you could have had.) (JK)
  • APR (Annual Percentage Rate): The yearly cost of borrowing money, shown in a percentage
  • Compound Interest: Interest earned overtime.
  • Principal: The Amount you owed before any interest was charged.
  • Late Fee: A charge you owe in result of a late payment.
  • Loan Term: The amount of time you have to repay a loan.
  • Overdraft: When you spent more than you have in your bank account, sometimes resulting in a fee. Some banks reverse the fee after money is deposited in your account, but it is usally case by case basis.
